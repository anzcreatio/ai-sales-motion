# W1 test 01: run notes (3 Nov 2026)

## Ambiguous, impossible or contradictory instructions in W1
1. **"Every research claim has a source and a date" vs reality.** Many usable sources show no date (job ads, vendor docs, exec profiles, some iTnews snippets). Rule 1 says "no source = no claim" but is silent on *undated* sources. I kept them and marked "date not shown". W1 should say whether undated = allowed with a flag, or dropped.
2. **"Prefer the last 18 months"** collided with the best contact-centre evidence (iTnews/iTWire, Dec 2024, ~23 months old). No rule for when older is the only source. I used it and flagged "older".
3. **Attendee names withheld vs "research the attendees".** Public sources name the person in the Head of Contact Centre role. I described them by title only and asked the SC to check it's the same person. W1 doesn't say whether to name people the handoff didn't name.
4. **Output 2 is "attendees" only**, but the people who probably sign (COO) and own AI risk (data & AI lead) aren't invited. I added them as "Not invited" rows. W1 should allow or forbid that explicitly (Output 10 half-covers it).
5. **Hypotheses "4 owned by the people attending"** is hard when one attendee is unnamed ("maybe someone from Digital") and the IT attendee rarely owns a pain. Result: 3 of 4 hypotheses sit with the Head of Contact Centre. Is that OK, or must the 4 be spread across attendees?
6. **Output 8 title** says "[organisations like yours]" but the slide must not reveal research. "Customer-owned banks like yours" is fine; W1 could give the allowed level of specificity.
7. **Rule 2 "nothing is C except handoff quotes"** but H1–H3 mix C (BDR) evidence with I/A. The tag column has one slot; I used combined tags ("I + C (BDR)"). Needs a rule.
8. **Rule 7 "ranges with a source"**: good KPI sources for Australian banks were not findable in 1–2 searches; the ones found are vendor blogs. W1 doesn't say whether a weak source beats "our estimate". I cited and labelled them "weak".
9. **Opportunity type "Both" vs track "P+A"**: naming differs between handoff (CRM / AI Studio / Both) and W1/card (P / A / P+A). Minor; mapping was obvious.

## What took the most effort
- **Disambiguation:** "Great Southern Bank" also = Great Southern Bancorp (Missouri, NASDAQ: GSBC). About a third of results were the US bank, and one full search (Fiserv core banking) was wasted on it. W1 should tell the researcher to check the country/ABN first.
- **Blocked sources:** the bank's own investor presentation and executive page returned 403; BusinessWire 403. The NiCE case study PDF only became readable by extracting it locally. The strategy pillars are cited via a search summary, not a read of the PDF.
- **Finding the incumbent and its AI roadmap** (NiCE/Optus, AutoSummary) mattered most: it reshapes H2 and the competitor angle. W1 doesn't explicitly ask "what does the incumbent already promise for the trigger?". Worth adding.

## What I couldn't find
- The name of the "older CRM"; any confirmed Microsoft Dynamics/Copilot use (only an M365 job ad).
- An AI policy or AI risk owner; who currently holds the CIO role.
- A dated, primary source for the core-to-cloud roadmap item (TCS BaNCS on AWS).
- Good Australian bank contact-centre benchmarks (AHT, FCR, containment). ACXPA reports exist but aren't open.
- Any public statement from an Enterprise Architect or the Digital team.

## Notable finding for the SC (test value)
Public data says service levels are good (80% of calls in 30 s; fastest new-business wait of Australian banks in Q4 2024). The BDR quote says customers "wait too long". The pack tells the SC to probe it rather than assert it. That is exactly the kind of catch W1 should make.

## Reading time
~2,300 words, mostly tables. **About 10–12 minutes** for a human to read; ~6–8 minutes to *check* it if the SC skims sources. Slightly over the 10-minute target. The source links inside tables add bulk; moving sources to footnote numbers would bring it under 10.
