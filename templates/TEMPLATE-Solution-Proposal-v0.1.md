# Solution Proposal: template v0.1

> **Customer-facing.** Sent ≤48 h after the value demo. Generated by W5 from the Discovery Brief, the demo recording and
> outcome notes, the differentiator catalogue and the Industry Pack; curated by the SC; checked by the AE.
> **Built on:** Command of the Message (value structure) · Shipley 4-box (executive summary) · Great Demo! (Critical
> Business Issue, Delta, Critical Date) · Forrester TEI (value case). See `research/proposal-handover-frameworks-2026-11-03.md`.
>
> **Writing rules**
> 1. **It must work without us in the room.** Buyers spend a small share of their buying time with suppliers (Gartner:
>    17%), so the champion will forward this to people we never met. Every section stands on its own.
> 2. **Their words first.** Hot buttons and pains are quoted and credited ("As Megan put it…"). Use their terms, not ours.
> 3. **Outcome before product.** No product names in headings. No feature lists.
> 4. **Honest about what's assumed.** Evidence tags become plain words: Confirmed → "you told us" · Inferred → "we
>    understand" · Assumed → "we've assumed, and will confirm with you".
> 5. **Claims only from the differentiator catalogue**, attributed as it says. Nothing from its "don't use" list.
>    AI governance statements only as the governance pack allows. Prices only from the official price book.
> 6. **Never knock a competitor by name.** Show what matters to them; let them compare.
> 7. Body ≤ 5,000 words (~10 pages). Plain English (local spelling). Screens from the demo, not stock images; if none
>    were captured, leave a "[Screen: …]" placeholder for the SC.
> 8. Plan durations and pilot lengths not agreed with the customer are labelled "proposed".

## Cover
**Title:** "[The outcome] for [Customer]", e.g. "Home loan decisions in under five days for Harbourline".
Prepared for (names) · prepared by (AE, SC) · date · version · "Confidential".

## 1. Executive summary (one page, Shipley 4-box)
| Box | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Your priorities** (their hot buttons, in *their* priority order, credited) | 2–4 bullets, their words |
| **2. What changes** (for each hot button: benefit → how → proof) | One line each; benefit first |
| **3. Why us** (the 2–3 differentiators that matter for *these* priorities) | One line each, with one proof point |
| **4. The value and the next step** | Conservative headline value (from §7) + one concrete next step with a date |

## 2. What we heard
- **Today (Before Scenario):** how it works now, in their words, with the journey picture from the demo.
- **What it costs you (Negative Consequences):** quoted, with numbers as ranges.
- **The goal at risk (Critical Business Issue)** and **why now / by when (Critical Date)**.
End with: "If we've got any of this wrong, tell us. Everything below depends on it."

## 3. Where you want to be (Positive Business Outcomes and Metrics)
| Outcome | Metric | Today | Target | Who owns it | Basis (you told us / we understand / assumed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 4. What any solution must do (Required Capabilities)
4–7 capabilities, each tied to an outcome in §3. Written vendor-neutral, but chosen so the ones only Creatio does well
are there (from the differentiator catalogue). Example: "Business teams can change approval limits themselves, the same day."

## 5. How we'd do it
**One heading per hot button, using their exact words.** Under each:
- **What changes for [persona]** (the benefit, first)
- **How it works** (2–4 sentences + 1–2 screens from the demo)
- **What you saw in the demo** (the proof moment they witnessed, incl. any live change made at their request)
- **Proof point** (catalogue only: analyst, customer story, attributed)

Include, where relevant: **AI that acts, with your people in control** (what the agent does alone vs what a person
approves; audit trail; only governance statements the governance pack allows) and **how it fits your systems**
(their named systems stay; the integration approach in one picture).

## 6. Why Creatio, for you
Three or four differentiators, chosen by their pains (core set: D1 + D3, plus D2 for platform deals, D5 for AI deals;
both for combined deals where one platform was a priority; manufacturing always D5). Where the catalogue has no proof
point, the proof is what they saw in the demo.
| What matters to you | What we do differently | Proof |
|---|---|---|

## 7. The value case (Forrester TEI-style, conservative)
- **Benefits** (one row per outcome): driver × volume × value per unit. **Risk adjustment by evidence:** you told us → −10% ·
  we understand → −25% · assumed → −50% *(our convention)*. Show risk-adjusted **and** unadjusted.
- **Costs** (new spend only): Creatio licences (plan, products, **AI Studio + AI credit package**) from the official price
  book · partner implementation as a **band** · internal effort (estimate).
- **Flexibility** (listed, **not counted**): e.g. the next AI use cases, other teams on the same platform.
- **Risks**: what could reduce the benefit, and how the plan handles it.
- **3-year view** (10% discount rate) with NPV, payback, and the **risk-adjusted figure as the headline**. Without
  confirmed prices, headline the risk-adjusted benefits "before costs" and show costs as placeholders.
- **Value per unit** (e.g. loaded hourly cost) comes from the customer if given, otherwise the Industry Pack default, stated as an assumption.
- Close with: "These are our estimates from what you told us. We'd like to rebuild them with your numbers."

## 8. Delivery approach
- **Phased roadmap:** Phase 1 (a first live outcome in weeks), later phases. One picture.
- **Who does what:** customer, implementation partner, Creatio.
- **How change stays fast after go-live:** business-owned rules and no-code changes (D1, D4); a governance rhythm.
- **AI rollout:** the measured pilot (task, weeks, KPIs, exit criteria) for any AI scope.

| Step | Owner (customer / Creatio / partner) | Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
From here to decision, contract and Phase 1 go-live. Includes the paper process (procurement, security review, legal).
