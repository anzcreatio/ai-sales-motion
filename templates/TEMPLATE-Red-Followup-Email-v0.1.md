# Red follow-up email: template v0.1

> **When:** the go/no-go is Red (process §5.3). Sent by the AE **within 24 h** of the discovery, instead of the
> "What we heard" one-pager. **Drafted by W2** (Output 3, Red variant); the AE records the personal intro and sends it.
> **Goal:** keep the door open at almost no SC cost, and get the 2–3 answers that would change the score.
> **Tone:** grateful, short, no pressure. They gave us 30 minutes; we give them something useful back.

## Rules
1. **≤ 150 words** in the body, before the questions.
2. **Their words** for the pain, quoted once. No product names in the subject line.
3. **2–3 questions only**: the missing items that made it Red (from W2 Output 2), phrased as helpful, answerable in
   one line. Never "who's the economic buyer?"; ask "who else would want a say if you went ahead?"
4. **One video link**, with the chapter that matches their pain named. Never a deck or a datasheet.
5. **No prices**, no claims outside the catalogue, no AI/data statement outside "can say".
6. **Never send a Red email** that pretends we built something for them.

## Email 1 (day 0)

**Subject:** [Their pain in their words], a short look, as promised

> Hi [first name],
>
> Thanks for the time on [day]. You mentioned [their pain, quoted: "…"], and that [the consequence they named].
>
> Here's a 5-minute look at how [organisations like yours] handle it in Creatio: [video link]
> The part on **[chapter name]** (from [mm:ss]) is closest to what you described.
>
> [Optional: 30-second personal intro: [link]]
>
> Before we suggest anything specific, it would help to know:
> 1. [Question on the missing item, e.g. "Is fixing this on anyone's plan for the next 12 months?"]
> 2. [Question, e.g. "Who else would want a say if you went ahead?"]
> 3. [Optional question, e.g. "Roughly how many [cases/applications] a month go through this?"]
>
> A one-line answer to any of these is plenty. If it's a fit, we'll show it working on your own process next time.
>
> [AE name] · [title] · [phone] · [booking link]

## Email 2 (day 7, only if no reply)

**Subject:** Re: [same subject]

> Hi [first name], one question from my last note, in case it got buried: [the single most useful question].
> If now's not the right time, no problem. Tell me when to check back.

Then stop. No reply after email 2 → nurture (marketing), and log it in the pilot sheet.

## Personal intro (30–60 s, AE, optional but recommended)

Recorded on a laptop or phone. Camera on, plain background.
1. "Hi [name], [AE] from Creatio. Thanks again for [day]." (5 s)
2. "You said [their pain, their words]. That's common in [industry], and the video shows how others deal with it." (15 s)
3. "Watch the [chapter] part. Then if you can answer the questions in the email, we'll know whether it's worth
   showing you this on your own process." (15 s)

## Example (fictional)
**Subject:** "Too many hands on every complaint", a short look, as promised
> Hi Sam, thanks for the time on Tuesday. You mentioned "every complaint goes through four people before anyone
> answers", and that some customers wait a week. Here's a 5-minute look at how service teams handle this in Creatio:

> specific: 1. Is fixing this on anyone's plan for the next 12 months? 2. Who else would want a say if you went ahead?
> A one-line answer is plenty. — Priya
