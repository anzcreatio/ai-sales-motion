# Frameworks behind the Solution Proposal and Partner Handover Pack: verification

**Date:** 3 Nov 2026 · **Scope:** verify the frameworks cited in `SC-MOTION-PROCESS-v0.5.md` §12 and §5, and extract what is useful for the two pre-contract presales documents.
**Method:** about 22 web searches plus page reads. Owner sites were preferred; where an owner page could not be read (Gartner returned 403/504), a secondary source is named and the quality is lowered.

**Quality key:** **Strong** = framework owner or authoritative body · **Medium** = reputable practitioner, press, training or reference site · **Weak** = vendor blog or marketing.
"Memory" = from prior knowledge, not confirmed in this session. Treat these as unverified.

## Implications for the Solution Proposal template

The proposal is read mostly when we are absent: buyers spend about 17% of their time with suppliers, and 6–10 people must agree (F4, F5). So it must work as a **consensus document the champion forwards**, not a brochure. Recommended sections, mapped to the v0.4 outline:

1. **Executive summary (1 page; Shipley 4-box, F1).** Their buying vision in their words → the **top 3 hot buttons in priority order, attributed** ("You told us…") → one benefit line per hot button → **one** headline outcome (risk-adjusted) → discriminators in one line → one realistic next step (the MAP milestone). No product names in the first paragraph.
2. **What we heard (CoM Before Scenario + Negative Consequences, A5).** Their current journey, the break points (Confirmed quotes) and the consequence of doing nothing. This is the written **Mantra** (A7) and the CoM "What We've Heard" step (A8). Keep evidence tags visible: Confirmed = fact, Inferred = "we understand", Assumed = listed assumption.
3. **Where you want to be (After Scenario + PBOs + Metrics), per persona.** Keep the "future state per persona" section, but write it as **PBO → Metric (from → to) per persona** (C3 "Delta"). Cover 3–5 personas, not all 6–10. Add a line on what each persona must believe to agree (consensus creation, F6).
4. **What it takes (Required Capabilities).** A neutral list of what *any* solution must do. Our differentiators should already be in this list through the discovery questions (trap-setting, A9). This section is missing from the v0.4 outline; add it. Evaluators reuse it as their decision criteria (MEDDPICC D, B3).
5. **How Creatio does it (solution mapped to hot buttons).** One subsection per hot button, **using the hot button verbatim as the heading** (F1), in the order **Benefit → How → Proof** (F2). Proof = the demo moment they saw (screenshot + "you saw this on 28 Oct") + a reference or customer metric. Mark the build level (L2/L3) honestly.
6. **Why Creatio for you (Differentiators + Proof Points).** At most 3 discriminators from the catalogue, each tied to a required capability and with one proof point. A claim without proof is an assertion (F3). Do not attack competitors (A1). "Competition" includes doing nothing and building in-house (B4), so address both.
7. **Value case (TEI-style lite, G1–G4).** A table of benefit lines, each with input · source · evidence tag; **show unadjusted and risk-adjusted values**. Haircut by evidence tag (suggested: Confirmed −10%, Inferred −25%, Assumed −50%, or exclude). Costs include licences + **partner implementation as an indicative band** + the customer's internal effort, all incremental. 3-year horizon, stated discount rate, payback. **Headline = conservative (risk-adjusted) case.** Add a "Change these numbers" note inviting their own inputs. Flexibility (future use cases, e.g. AI Studio) is listed but **not counted in ROI**.
8. **Delivery approach.** Partner-led, phased. State that **detailed scope and effort are set in the partner's discovery / fit-to-standard phase** (H4). Show a first phase that matches the demo scope.
9. **Assumptions** (all Assumed and Inferred items as a numbered list the customer can tick) and **Mutual action plan** (E1). The MAP must include buyer-side steps: economic buyer review, **paper process** (security, legal, procurement; B4), the partner discovery kickoff, and a target go-live to work back from. Every row has an owner, a date and a status.

Drop or avoid: a long "about Creatio" section (move it to an appendix), feature lists not tied to a hot button (Cohan's "Buying It Back", D2), and an unadjusted ROI headline.

## Implications for the Partner Handover Pack template

The pack is the equivalent of a **solution scope document** (SAP Prepare) and the starting point for a **charter**, not a blueprint (H3–H5). The partner turns it into a blueprint and backlog in its own discovery. Recommended sections:

1. **Deal context.** Customer, industry, why now, **MEDDPICC snapshot** (EB, champion, decision criteria, decision and paper process status, competition including do-nothing), stakeholder map with stance. Mark which stakeholders the partner has not met.
2. **Value as sold (the commitments).** The hot buttons, PBOs and **Metrics (from → to)** exactly as in the Proposal, with evidence tags. These are the success criteria the partner must later prove (A10, H1). Add a "**Commitments made in the sale**" list: anything said in the demo or Proposal, including timelines and "yes, it can do that" answers (H1).
3. **Scope as sold.** In-scope journeys and processes, **explicit out-of-scope**, phase-1 versus later. One line per item linked to the Proposal section, so there is no contradiction (Gate C).
4. **Conceptual solution (solution outline).** Rename it from "blueprint". Cover capabilities per journey, Creatio components and packs, build-pattern IDs with buildability ratings, and a **landscape sketch** (systems, direction of data, coexistence D5). Stay at charter level (H5).
5. **Reference demo and demo-only items.** What was configured (L2/L3/L4), what was **simulated** (integrations, data, AI on simulated data), the stand and package location, the click path and recording links. For the AI track, include the agent spec and test set.
6. **Assumptions and open questions by SBR area (H3).** Use the Microsoft SBR topics adapted to Creatio as the fixed headings: **business process · application/configuration · data & migration · integration · security & access · AI/intelligence · test · environments & ALM · programme/governance**. Under each heading, list every item tagged **Confirmed / Inferred / Assumed / Unknown**, and classify it as an **assertion / risk / issue** (SBR vocabulary). This becomes the **partner discovery agenda** (fit-to-standard style, H4).
7. **Risks.** Delivery, adoption, data, integration, commercial and AI-policy risks, each with an owner and a mitigation.
8. **Commercial constraints.** Budget signal, licence assumptions, target dates, paper-process status, and anything promised on price or terms. Missing from the v0.4 outline; add it.
9. **Indicative effort band + confidence.** Keep it, but label it **non-binding, pre-fit-to-standard** (H4). Show the drivers that move the band (the number of integrations, data volume, the number of Assumed items).
10. **Handover check.** A 30-minute SC → partner walkthrough. The partner opens its customer kickoff with a "what we heard" slide built from section 2 (H2).
